# ASTRA CRYPTO FEDERATION Ω — prospective pilot protocol
28 November 2026. Research proposal, not a consortium agreement. No participating institution is confirmed.
Baseline: WAN experimental proof run 46085852230 (two GitHub-hosted VMs, 4/3 split). New PQ consensus test 46116281263, status not yet validated at time of drafting.
 
## Scope
Seven equally weighted validators, preferably seven separate organizations, minimum four administrative domains (2+2+2+1); no domain may operate more than two validators. Five of seven voting power to finalize. Each organization generates its own validator key locally and separately controls its incident attestation key. No central generation or copying of private keys. Exchange public keys, signed proof of possession, signed agreed genesis digest and contact for incident escalation through authenticated channels. Each operator records its own audit and attests administrative and hosting domain. Separate consensus and incident signing keys; no production payment data, no actual funds. Institutional acceptance and test authorization mandatory before onboarding. Avoid claim of physical independence without infrastructure evidence.
 
## Network model and acceptance
Candidate logical graph: cycle of seven plus distance-two edges (14 undirected links). Each validator has four peers. No single proxy, account, provider, or route may constitute a shared failure domain for all cross-domain paths. Independently managed encrypted P2P paths; document dependencies, shared AS/provider, NAT, credentials, DNS and failure domains. Audit measured paths, not just logical edges.
Exhaustive enumeration: graph remains connected in 15/15 cases of zero node loss plus at most one edge loss; 77/77 cases of one node loss plus at most one surviving edge loss; 161/161 cases of two node losses plus at most one surviving edge loss. This is purely a graph result and assumes independent logical link failures, not evidence of physical network availability.
 
## Predefined tests
1. At least 1000 fictional payments in each of 3 phases: baseline, targeted payment-algorithm revocation, family-level payment-algorithm revocation. Distinct signed policy decisions and no acceptance of revoked signatures in their scoped epoch. Measure p50/p95/p99, throughput, certificate bytes and bandwidth per operator.
2. Remove each administrative domain in turn; require no conflicting finalized blocks and eventual progress when five voting validators remain and communication stabilizes. Observe a preselected missing proposer and actual nonzero-round signed block.
3. Disable each transport failure domain independently; demonstrate alternate paths in logs and successful finalization. Partition into 4/3; no finalization on either side while disconnected; reconnect and converge. Record observation windows, height traces and signed commits. 
4. Controls: conflicting proposals, duplicate signer, altered block hash/chain ID/round/signature, old epoch, tampered incident, insufficient attestations, stale keys. Byzantine equivocation only with disposable test keys in an isolated authorized testnet. Safety: no conflicting commits for same height. Test liveness separately under partial synchrony; record timeouts and failures.
5. Native post-quantum consensus: genesis accepts only ml_dsa_65, all public validator keys and commit signatures verified independently, negative controls rejected. Compare to Ed25519 baseline at same topology, workload and host resources. PQ signatures do not establish PQ transport confidentiality. Application revocation of ML-DSA does not revoke consensus keys; family-wide consensus compromise remains a separate unresolved key-agility problem.
6. All public evidence includes genesis, signed headers, public keys, transaction inclusion, snapshots and hashes, per-domain logs, transport dependency list. No secrets. No claim of independent governance without separate institutional custody attestation; no claim of industrial qualification or formal proof.
